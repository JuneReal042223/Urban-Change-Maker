About

A scholar-practitioner shaped by housing policy, community realities, and public service.

Positioning Statement

I connect rigorous interdisciplinary analysis with senior public-sector leadership to advance housing systems that are affordable, participatory, climate-aware, and socially just.

My work sits at the intersection of social housing policy and finance, community-driven upgrading, participatory planning, pro-poor climate adaptation, urban housing vulnerability, gender and intersectionality in housing, and multi-level governance in the Global South.

At the Social Housing Finance Corporation, I lead strategy and policy development for community-driven housing programs, including program enhancement, project approvals, coordination with local planning units, implementation oversight, and high-level intergovernmental and multi-stakeholder engagement.

I bring the perspective of a practitioner, a lawyer, an onmental planner, a researcher, a lecturer, and a public writer. This combination allows me to translate complex urban problems into practical, evidence-informed, and community-sensitive solutions.

Educational Focus

Inclusive urban development means building cities where housing, safety, dignity, and opportunity are not reserved for the privileged few.

👥

Community-driven upgrading

Residents are not merely beneficiaries of housing programs. They are planners, organizers, knowledge-holders, and rights-holders whose participation can improve the legitimacy and durability of urban interventions.

🛡

Resilient social housing

Resilience requires more than disaster-safe structures. It requires secure tenure, affordable finance, community capacity, climate-aware planning, livelihood access, and long-term estate management.

🌏

Global South perspective

Urban housing vulnerability in the Global South is shaped by rapid urbanization, informality, unequal land markets, climate risk, public finance constraints, and fragmented governance.

🏢

Institutional reform

Better housing outcomes depend on better institutions: clearer mandates, accountable implementation, smarter financing, participatory planning, and coordination across government, civil society, academia, and communities.
